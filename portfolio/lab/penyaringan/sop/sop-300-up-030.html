TUJUAN

Untuk memastikan ujian identifikasi dan kandungan Menthol, Camphor, Methyl Salicylate dan Thymol dalam produk tradisional menggunakan Gas Chromatography-Mass Spectrometer (GCMS) dilaksanakan dengan cekap dan berkesan.

SKOP

Prosedur ini hendaklah digunakan untuk menjalankan ujian identifikasi dan kandungan Menthol, Camphor, Methyl Salicylate dan Thymol dalam produk tradisional menggunakan Gas Chromatography-Mass Spectrometer (GCMS).

6.2 Preparation of Reference Standard
6.2.1 Preparation of Internal Standard (IS) solution (0.4 mg/ml or 400 µg/ml)

Liquid form: Pipette 10 µl of eugenol reference standard into a 25 ml amber volumetric flask. Dissolve and dilute to volume with methanol. Filter the solution through a 0.45 µm nylon syringe filter into an amber vial.

Powder form: Weigh 10 mg of eugenol reference standard into a 25 ml amber volumetric flask. Dissolve and dilute to volume with methanol. Filter the solution through a 0.45 µm nylon syringe filter into an amber vial.

6.2.2 Preparation of Individual Standard Stock Solution (0.4 mg/ml or 400 µg/ml)

Weigh 8 mg each of thymol, menthol and camphor into separate 20 ml amber volumetric flasks.

Dissolve and dilute to volume with methanol.

Filter the solution through a 0.45 µm nylon syringe filter into amber vials.

For methyl salicylate: Pipette 7 µl of methyl salicylate into a 20 mL amber volumetric flask and repeat steps ii and iii.

Preparation of Mixed Working Standard Solution (0.1 mg/ml or 100 µg/ml).

Pipette 12.5 ml of each standard stock solution into an amber 50 ml vial.

Vortex the vial for 1 minute.

6.2.4 Preparation of Mixed Working Standard Solution for Calibration Curve

Prepare 1 set of working standard solution for the calibration curve from the mixed working standard solution (0.1 mg/ml). Dilute the solution to obtain five (5) concentrations levels of 3 µg/ml, 10 µg/ml, 20 µg/ml, 40 µg/ml and 80 µg/ml.

Add the IS solution to each concentration before diluting to volume with methanol.

6.2.5 System Suitability Solution

For system suitability test (SST), use the Level 3 concentration (20 µg/ml of each compound) as the system suitability solution.

6.2.6 Preparation of Limit of Detection (LOD) solution (0.001 mg/ml or 1 µg/ml)

Pipette 50 µl of mixed working standard solution into 5 ml volumetric flask and add 1000 µl of internal standard (IS) solution.

Mix and dilute to volume with methanol.

6.2.7 Limit of quantitation (LOQ) solution (0.003 mg/ml or 3 µg/ml)

Use the Level 1 concentration (3 µg/ml of each compound) as the LOQ solution.

6.3 Preparation of Sample

Patch / Medicated Plaster

Cut the sample into small pieces.

Weigh 100 mg of the sample and add 15 ml of methanol into a 50 mL conical flask (duplicate preparation: Replicates A and B).

Vortex the samples for 1 minute.

Sonicate with heat at 55°C for 15 minutes.

Cool the solution to room temperature.

Add 5 ml of IS solution and 5 ml of methanol.

Filter the sample solution through a 0.45 µm filter into a 2 ml amber vial.

6.3.2 Liniment / Ointment / Cream

Sampling the sample according to PKKK/300/UP/001.

Weigh 100 mg of the sample and add 15 ml of methanol in a 25 ml amber volumetric flask (duplicate preparation: Replicates A and B).

Vortex the sample for 1 minute.

Sonicate with heat at 55°C for 15 minutes.

Cool the solution to room temperature.

Add 5 ml of IS solution and dilute to volume with methanol.

Filter the sample solution through a 0.45 µm filter into a 2 mL amber vial.

6.3.3 Sample Dilution

Perform appropriate dilutions if the sample concentration exceeds the linear range of the calibration curve.

Document the step-by-step dilution preparation in the worksheet.

Standard injection and calibration curve

Inject calibration curve solution (Level 1 – 5) to generate linear calibration curve.

Generate a calibration curve by plotting concentration against area, and determine the linearity equation along with the correlation coefficient (R2).

This step (ii) can be performed using the instrument’s software (GCMSsolution).

Ensure that the correlation coefficient (R²) is equal to or greater than 0.995. If the correlation does not meet this requirement, re-inject the working standard solution until the desired criteria are met.

6.4.5 Internal Quality Control (IQC)

Inject 0.5 µl of LOD solution after every 5 samples & at the end of analysis.

Acceptance criteria for Internal Quality Control Check (IQC):

The percentage difference of retention time for LOD solution should not be more than 3 % for every batch of 5 samples.

6.4.6 Interpretation of result

Identification

The identification of the target compound is considered positive when the following criteria are met:

Retention time

The retention time of the target compound in the sample chromatogram corresponds to the retention time in the chromatogram of the working standard.

Mass Spectrum Confirmation

Confirmation is based on the detection of the target ion and reference ions.

Reference ions are monitored as relative ion intensity, with the maximum permissible tolerance defined in Table 8:

Table 8: Tolerance for reference ion intensity for identification of menthol, camphor, methyl salicylate and thymol

Reporting Results

NOT DETECTED: The compound is reported as NOT DETECTED when the peak area ratio of the compound to the internal standard (IS) in the sample solution is below the peak area ratio of the standard solution at the limit of detection (LOD, 1 µg/mL).

BELOW QUANTITATION LIMIT: The compound is reported as BELOW QUANTITATION LIMIT (3 µg/mL) when the peak area ratio of the compound to IS in the sample solution is below the peak area ratio of the standard solution at the limit of quantitation (LOQ, 3 µg/mL)

Quantifiable Amounts: If the peak area ratio of the compound to IS in the sample solution exceeds the peak area ratio of the standard solution at the LOQ level, the compound's concentration must be calculated using the calibration curve extrapolation as outlined in section B. Assay.

Assay

The amounts of menthol, camphor, methyl salicylate, and thymol are determined using the extrapolation of the calibration curve.

Therefore, the final amount of each compound in the sample is calculated and reported as percent weight / weight (% w/w).
