6.1 Material and Equipment

Instrument: Gas Chromatography Mass Spectrometer (GCMS)

Column: BP-20 (Wax), with dimension of 30 m x 0.25 mm and particle size of 0.25 µm or equivalent.

Analytical balance and microbalance

Vortex Mixer

Sonicator

Reference standard: Primary or secondary RS of equivalent.

Diethylene Glycol (Cas No.: 111-46-6), Density: 1.118 g/mL

Ethylene Glycol (Cas No.: 107-21-1), Density: 1.113 g/mL

Chemical:

Methanol

Glassware and consumables:

Conical flask

Amber volumetric flask

Disposable syringe without needle, 5 mL

Nylon solvent filter, 0.45µm

Amber glass screw cap vial, 2 mL

6.2 Preparation of Reference Standard
6.2.1 Preparation of Mixed Standard Stock Solution (EG: 222.6 µg/mL, DEG 223.6 µg/mL)

Pipette 10 µL (equivalent to 11.13 mg) of Ethylene Glycol and 10 µL (equivalent to 11.18 mg) of Diethylene Glycol reference standard into a 50 mL amber volumetric flask.

Add about 10 mL of methanol and vortex/sonicate at ambient temperature to mix.

Make up to volume with methanol.

Filter the solution through a 0.45 µm nylon syringe filter into amber vials.

6.2.2 Preparation for Calibration Curve

Prepare 1 set of mix working standard solution for the calibration curve from the mixed standard stock solution. Dilute the solution to obtain five (5) concentrations levels of 6 µg/mL, 8 µg/mL, 10 µg/mL, 12 µg/mL and 14 µg/mL, into a 5mL volumetric flask.

Vortex for 30 seconds to mix.

Refer to the table below for preparation details:

Table 1: Preparation of working standard solution for calibration curve.

6.2.3 System Suitability Solution

For system suitability test (SST), use the Level 3 concentration (10 µg/mL of each compound) as the system suitability solution.

Preparation of Limit of Detection (LOD) solution (EG: 2.00 µg/mL, DEG: 2.01 µg/mL).

Pipette 45 µL of mixed standard stock solution into a 5 mL volumetric flask.

Make up to volume with methanol.

Vortex to mix.

Limit of quantitation (LOQ) solution (0.006 mg/mL or 6 µg/mL)

Use the Level 1 concentration (6 µg/mL of each compound) as the LOQ solution.

Qualitative Screening Test (Identification)

For the qualitative screening test (Identification), prepare only LOD, Level 1 (LOQ) and Level 3 (SST) concentration solutions.

6.3 Preparation of Sample

Sample processing is carried out according to document PKKK/300/UP/001 (Proses Persampelan).

Liquid / Syrup / Suspension

Lightly shake the sample bottle to mix sample thoroughly.

Accurately pipette 1 mL of sample and add about 50 mL of methanol into a 100 mL volumetric flask (duplicate preparation: Replicates A and B).

Sonicate the solution for 5 minutes.

Cool the solution to room temperature.

Make up to volume with methanol.

Filter the sample solution through a 0.45 µm filter into a 2 mL amber vial.

6.3.3 Sample Dilution (for quantitative assay purposes only)

Perform appropriate dilutions if the sample concentration exceeds the linear range of the calibration curve.

Document the step-by-step dilution preparation in the worksheet.

Standard injection and calibration curve

Inject calibration curve solution (Level 1 – 5) to generate linear calibration curve. For the qualitative screening test (Identification), calibration curve acceptance criteria do not apply.

Generate a calibration curve by plotting concentration against area, and determine the linearity equation along with the correlation coefficient (R2).

This step (ii) can be performed using the instrument’s software.

Ensure that the correlation coefficient (R²) is equal to or greater than 0.9950. If the correlation does not meet this requirement, re-inject the working standard solution until the desired criteria are met.

The typical retention time for EG is around 7.00-7.50 minutes and DEG is around 14.00-14.50 minutes.

6.4.5 Internal Quality Control (IQC)

Inject 1.0 µL of LOD solution after every 5 samples & at the end of analysis.

Acceptance criteria for Internal Quality Control Check (IQC):

The percentage difference of retention time for LOD solution should not be more than 3 % for every batch of 5 samples.

6.4.6 Interpretation of result

Identification

The identification of the target compound is considered positive when the following criteria are met:

Retention time

The retention time of the target compound in the sample chromatogram corresponds to the retention time in the chromatogram of the working standard.

Mass Spectrum Confirmation

Confirmation is based on the detection of the target and reference ions.

Reference ions are monitored as relative ion intensity, with the maximum permissible tolerance defined in Table 6:

Table 6: Tolerance for reference ion intensity for identification of ethylene glycol and diethylene glycol

Reporting Results

NOT DETECTED: The compound is reported as NOT DETECTED when the peak area of the target compound in the sample solution is below the peak area of the standard solution at the limit of detection (LOD) level, 2 µg/mL (equivalent to 0.02% v/v in sample).

BELOW QUANTITATION LIMIT: The compound is reported as BELOW QUANTITATION LIMIT in the sample when the peak area of the target compound in the sample solution is below the peak area in standard solution at the limit of quantitation (LOQ) level, 6 µg/mL (equivalent to 0.06% v/v in sample).

For discordant replicate sample analyses, the following approach shall be applied:

▹ i) BORDERLINE LOD RESULTS

Where one result of the peak area is below the LOD and the other is equal to or above the LOD, the arithmetic mean (average) of the replicate peak area shall be used for interpretation.

If the average result of the peak area is below the LOD, the sample shall be reported as < LOD 0.02% v/v.

If the average result of the peak area is equal to or above the LOD but below the LOQ, the analyte shall be considered detected but < LOQ 0.06% v/v. No repeat analysis is required.

ii) BORDERLINE LOQ RESULTS

Where one result of the peak area is below the LOQ and the other is equal to or above the LOQ, a repeat analysis using a freshly prepared test portion shall be performed with a calibration curve to confirm whether the analyte is present at a quantifiable concentration before reporting the final result.

The final reported result shall be based on the outcome of the repeat analysis in accordance with the laboratory’s validated reporting procedure.

Quantifiable Amounts: If the peak area of the compound in the sample solution exceeds the peak area of the standard solution at the LOQ level, the compound's concentration must be calculated using the calibration curve extrapolation as outlined in section B. Assay.

Assay

The amounts of ethylene glycol and diethylene glycol are determined using the extrapolation of the calibration curve.

Therefore, the final amount of each compound in the sample is calculated and reported as percent volume / volume (% v/v).
