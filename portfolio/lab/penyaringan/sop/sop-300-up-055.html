1.0 Objective

◼ The test is performed to identify and quantitate the presence of Fluoride in toothpaste products by using Ion Chromatography.

2.0 Scope

◼ This method specifies a procedure for the identification and quantification of Fluoride in Toothpaste products.

3.0 Definition
3.1 HPLC : High Pressure Liquid Chromatography
3.2 Analyst : Pharmacist/Pharmacist Assistant/Science Officer

Procedure

Equipment/ Materials

High Pressure Liquid Chromatography with Ion Conductivity detector

Ultrapure water (Type I reagent grade, 18MΩ-cm resistivity)

Boric Acid

4-Hydroxybenzoic Acid

2,2-Bis(hydroxymethyl)-2,2’,2”-nitrilotriethanol (Bis-Tris)

0.2µm Vacuum Pump Filter

Ultrasonic Bath, pH meter, Electronic Analytical Balance, Volumetric

Flasks, Beakers, Measuring Cylinders, Pipettes

0.25µm nylon syringe filters

Solid Sodium Fluoride Reference Standard (NaF)

Blank Sample: *Verified Fluoride toothpaste sample matrix.

▹ *Verification done as explained in blank sample verification (6.4.3) to ensure sample matrix is Fluoride free with acceptable recovery prior to analysis.

Chromatographic Parameters

Test Method

Mobile Phase

Weigh about 2.21 g of p-hydroxybenzoic acid and 6.18 g of boric acid. Dissolve (use magnetic stirrer) them separately into 500 mL of diluent at 40 ºC for 90 minutes. Weigh about 1.338 g of ‘Bis-Tris’ and dissolve in 500 mL of diluent. Mix all three solutions and add up to 2 L with diluent. Filter with 0.2 µm vacuum filter prior to use.

Reference Standard Stock Solution

Weigh approximately 8.8578 mg of the NaF Reference Standard and make up to 20 mL with diluent. (200 ppm of Fluoride)

Standard Solution for Identification

Transfer 500 µL standard stock solution (6.3.2) to a 10 mL-volumetric flask. Add to volume with diluent and mix. (10 ppm)

Standard Solution for Calibration Curve,

STD1: Transfer 125 µL standard stock solution (6.3.2) to a 10 mL-

volumetric flask. Add to volume with diluent and mix. (2.5 ppm)

STD2: Transfer 250 µL standard stock solution (6.3.2) to a 10 mL-volumetric

flask. Add to volume with diluent and mix. (5 ppm)

STD3: Transfer 500 µL standard stock solution (6.3.2) to a 10 mL-volumetric

flask. Add to volume with diluent and mix. (10 ppm)

STD4: Transfer 1 mL standard stock solution (6.3.2) to a 10 mL-volumetric

flask. Add to volume with diluent and mix. (20 ppm)

STD5: Transfer 3 mL standard stock solution (6.3.2) to a 20 mL-volumetric

flask. Add to volume with diluent and mix. (30 ppm)

STD6: Transfer 2 mL standard stock solution (6.3.2) to a 10 mL-volumetric

flask. Add to volume with diluent and mix. (40 ppm)

Filter the solutions prior to injecting into the HPLC.

Note:

The weight of reference standard can be modified to minimize the usage of the reference standard.

For identification purposes, prepare only STD solution of 10 ppm.

STD 1 is the method’s Limit of Quantification (LOQ).

Blank Sample

Weigh approximately 0.2 g of the sample into a 50 mL volumetric flask and add 20 mL diluent. Sonicate for 30 minutes. Make up to volume with diluent and filter the solution prior to injecting into the HPLC.

QC Solution A (LOD, 1.25 ppm)

Transfer 125 µL of standard stock solution (6.3.2) into a 20 mL-volumetric flask containing 0.2 g Blank Sample. Add 20 mL diluent. Sonicate for 30 minutes. Make up to volume with diluent, mix and filter the solution prior to injecting into the HPLC.

QC Solution B (LOQ, 2.50 ppm)

Transfer 625 µL of standard stock solution (6.3.2) into a 50 mL-volumetric flask containing 0.2 g Blank Sample. Add 20 mL diluent. Sonicate for 30 minutes. Make up to volume with diluent, mix and filter the solution prior to injecting into the HPLC.

Sample Preparation

Weigh approximately 0.2 g of the sample into a 50 mL volumetric flask and add 20 mL diluent. Sonicate for 30 minutes. Make up to volume with diluent and filter the solution prior to injecting into the HPLC. Prepare in duplicates.

Note:

The weight of sample can be modified according to the availability and amount of the sample received.

For sample intended for use in children, approximately 0.5 g is weighed during sample preparation. This is to enable concentration of the sample to be calculated (above quantitation limit).

All solutions to be prepared freshly prior to analysis.

System Suitability Test (SST)

Identification test

Inject 20L Standard Solution (6.3.3) and determine:

Tailing factor (NMT 2.0)

Theoretical plates (NLT 1800)

Capacity Factor (within 1 to 5)

Determination test

Inject Six (6) replicates of 20L Standard Solution-STD4 (6.3.4) and determine:

Tailing factor (NMT 2.0)

Theoretical plates (NLT 1800)

Capacity Factor (within 1 to 5)

Retention Time RSD (NMT 2%)

Peak Area RSD (NMT 3%)

Blank Sample Verification

Inject Diluent, Blank Sample (6.3.5), QC Solution B (6.3.7), STD1 (6.3.4) and determine:

No Fluoride detected in the blank sample

Fluoride recovery from QC Solution B is within 80 - 120%.

Run the test in the following sequence:

Identification test:

▹ *QC Check solution A (6.3.6) is re-injected every six (6) sample solution (including duplicate sample) as a quality control measure.

Determination:

▹ *QC Check solution B (6.3.7)* is re-injected every six (6) sample solution (including duplicate sample) as a quality control measure. Fluoride recovery from QC Solution B is within 80 - 120%.

Interpretation

Identification test

Observe the peak from QC Solution A injected throughout the sequence and compare against the Retention Time (RT) of Reference STD3 (6.3.3). Percentage difference should be less than 3%.

6.6.1.2 The presence of Fluoride is confirmed and reported as positive/ ‘dikesan’ only when these two (2) criteria’s are met:

RT from the peak observed from sample solution with that obtained from Ref STD does not differ by ± 0.5min

The peak area or height of the sample solution is more than the peak area/height of the Detection Limit (QC Solution A).

Determination

Observe the peak from QC Solution B injected throughout the sequence and compare against the Average Retention Time (RT) of Reference STD4. Percentage difference should be less than 3%.
